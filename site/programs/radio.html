How The Observatory Works

observe first · interpret second
01 / ACQUIRE One fixed RF span

A receive-only HackRF captures its maximum 20 MHz span with Channel 1 as the logical center. A small digital tuning offset keeps the trusted carrier away from the hardware DC spike; the receiver does not scan between FRS channels.

02 / OBSERVE Twenty-two channel paths

All 22 carrier probes run continuously. Channel 1 audio stays hot; other NFM paths wake on carrier activity and remain open through evidence capture.

03 / ENVIRONMENT Impulses before the gate

A 51.2 µs pre-gate power path covers the full 20 MHz capture. Qualified events retain 0.4 seconds of aliased temporal IQ; RF-only identification is complete without microphones, while any available acoustic correlation remains optional secondary evidence.

04 / UNDERSTAND Ensemble interpretation

Candidate transcripts, descriptive audio, 50 Hz RF edge timing, completed-WAV envelope recovery, deterministic rules, and local-model reconciliation describe what happened. Per-press release chimes terminate PTT holds but never become Morse symbols.

05 / RESPOND Bounded incident policy

Only sufficient evidence becomes an incident. A single trusted PTT is preserved without a cascade; repeated signaling, voice, or SOS can escalate.

Alert Ladder

evidence determines response
Passive Observation Carrier or outside traffic

Recorded and available to the live monitor. Short non-trusted noise does not create an immediate prompt or phone reminder.

Trusted Incident Single Channel 1 PTT

Raises one durable local alert and one staged phone update with no repeat schedule. Acknowledgment cancels pending reminders transactionally; atomic scheduler leases prevent restart duplicates. An explicit high-confidence spoken test appears first, then may close after a seven-second minimum.

Escalated Incident Morse, directed voice, or SOS

Multiple verified holds receive bounded reminders. SOS uses a tempo-independent 1:2 test: dots are near 1/12 and dashes near 1/6 of total keyed time, with exactly nine ordered holds, separated clusters, high confidence, and the indexed release chimes. The ninth chime closes the sequence and preserves one protected combined WAV. Operator intent alone never creates an SOS decode.
